## 2. Founder decisions

Only choices that change what a client or regulator sees, or which method the product stands behind. Recommended option first. Each option: one sentence on what the consultant sees.

**D1. Which number does a department, job title, education level or location get in the drivers list, the chart, the PDF and the narrative?**
1. **Frequency-weighted deviation (recommended).** Every level, including the one that used to be hidden as the base, shows its unexplained effect relative to the company-wide average employee's level; adding a 3-person "Direction" department changes only that department's row (today's carve-out test: every untouched department moved by less than 1e-5; the intercept by 0.0006). Cost: an auditor replicating in Stata or R must apply the weighted restriction by hand from the formula printed in the PDF's methodology block; Stata `categorical()` and R `oaxaca` print the unweighted version.
2. **Unweighted deviation (Yun 2005 / Gardeazabal-Ugidos 2004; what Stata `categorical()`, R `oaxaca` and `ddecompose normalize_factors = TRUE` print).** Same screens, each level relative to the simple average of the levels; replicable to 1e-9 with standard tools today (§ 0 row 2), but carving a 20-person department out of a 1,700-person one re-signs every other department and moves the intercept 0.040 → 0.068, so a small reorganisation reads as a new finding.
3. **Variable-level only.** The drivers list shows one row "Department" and hides the intercept; no engine change, but the Department total is still base-dependent (−0.0129 vs −0.0008 from one relabel) and the consultant loses the per-level hotspot view.
4. **Stop presenting per-level unexplained numbers as findings.** Drivers list, chart and narrative drivers go; the product reports the aggregate gap, explained and unexplained totals, the explained-side breakdown (base-invariant) and the remedy. Nothing shown can be an identification artefact; the executive summary loses its centrepiece.

Under 1 and 2 the headline gap, explained and unexplained totals, remedy dollars and defensibility do not change. The intercept leaves every ranked list and appears once as the baseline difference.

**D2. What does the headline "Unexplained Gap" mean under the "Standard" method?**
1. **The baseline group's pay structure (recommended; app sends `GroupB`).** The headline becomes "what the compared group would earn under the baseline group's pay structure", the same number the remedy simulator shows at zero budget and the same direction as the s. 62 male earning curve (Jann 2008 eq. 5, p. 456; Oaxaca 1973's index-number remark); on the fixture it moves 1.6%, more on rosters with a larger characteristics gap.
2. **Pooled regression with a gender indicator (Jann 2008 `pooled`; Fortin 2008; recommended by Jann, Fortin and statsmodels).** The headline becomes the gender coefficient of one pooled regression, the number most pay-equity regressions report; the "Reference group" field stops driving the decomposition but still drives the remedy, so the headline and the remedy's zero-budget gap still differ.
3. **Keep today's number and name it ("the compared group's pay structure").** Nothing moves; the label now says the headline evaluates the baseline group's characteristics under the compared group's returns, and a reviewer will ask why.

**D3. When the compared group's characteristics lie outside the baseline group's range (or a class is too small), what does the consultant see?**
1. **A plain caveat on the result and on each extrapolated remedy row, never a block (recommended).** "For 60 of 60 compared employees the fair wage extends the baseline group's pay line beyond the range observed (education 8-11 vs 16-20 years); their amounts rest on that extension." Same sentence in the PDF; a class with fewer than 10 residual degrees of freedom carries "too few employees for a reliable interval"; nothing is refused.
2. **The same caveat, and the run requires an acknowledgement before OPTIMIZE.** The consultant must tick "I understand these amounts extrapolate" before a schedule is produced.
3. **Refuse the run above a threshold.** The dashboard shows "cannot compute: no overlap on Education" and the consultant has to change the model.
4. **Nothing (status quo).** The no-overlap roster keeps reporting −$10,560 per woman with p = 0.000 and no sentence.

**D4. What is the headline in quantile mode (p10 / p50 / p90)?**
1. **The actual percentile gap, with the decomposition shown as an approximation (recommended).** The card reads "Gap at the 90th percentile: 0.000 log points" (type-7 quantiles per group); below it "Model total 0.0001, explained X, unexplained Y" with a note when the two differ, and a warning when salaries are gridded ("38% of employees share the 90th-percentile salary; percentile results are unreliable on step grids").
2. **Keep the model total as the headline, renamed.** The card says "Model gap at the 90th percentile: 0.0001" and the tooltip explains it is a regression approximation; on the fixture the consultant still reads a 0.012 gap at p10 where the percentiles are equal.
3. **Hide quantile mode when salaries are gridded.** The quantile option greys out above a tie threshold with "not available for step-grid pay"; continuous rosters keep today's behaviour plus option 1's headline.

**D5. What does the "Statistical Health" card and the defensibility flag say?**
1. **Say what it is (recommended).** "92% of adjusted employees land inside the 95% pay range of comparable baseline-group employees"; "legal" and "robust" leave the copy, FR says "intervalle de prédiction", and the card gains the group-level line "after adjustment, the gender coefficient is 0.0003 (p = 0.61)" from the pooled test the frontier already computes.
2. **Remove the card and the MCP "defensibility" tool wording.** The consultant sees the remedy schedule and the verify result only.
3. **Keep the wording.** "All proposed adjustments fall within the 95% confidence interval of the Fair Wage model. This model is statistically robust." stays, and it keeps passing extrapolated amounts.

**D6. Projects saved before this change: what does the consultant see on reopening?**
1. **Stored results stay, marked (recommended).** The dashboard shows the saved numbers with "computed with the previous method (drivers unnormalised, headline under the compared group's structure); recompute to update", and the PDF cannot be regenerated without recomputing.
2. **Recompute on open.** The numbers change silently the first time the project is opened after the update.
3. **Both versions side by side** until the consultant picks one, for every reopened project.
