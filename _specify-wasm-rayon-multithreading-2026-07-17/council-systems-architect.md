## Findings (ranked)

### MAJOR-1 — INV-02 / AC-6 overclaims *cross-platform* byte-identity; RNG fix only buys *intra-platform* determinism

deterministic-rng line 22 (master criterion) and **AC-6** demand `sha256(json_native_seq) == sha256(json_native_4t) == sha256(json_wasm_seq)` — i.e. **native and wasm produce byte-identical JSON**. The whole deterministic-rng spec treats bit-identity as an RNG-ordering problem. It is only half the problem. The other half — cross-ISA floating-point reproducibility — is never addressed, and it is very likely fatal for the native↔wasm leg:

- `oaxaca_blinder/src/math/rif.rs:69` and `kde.rs:5` compute the Gaussian kernel with `.exp()`; `rif.rs:59`/`kde.rs:58` use `.powf(-0.2)` (Silverman bandwidth). `exp`/`powf` are transcendental — Rust dispatches them to the *platform libm*: system glibc libm on native x86-64 vs the pure-Rust/compiler-builtins libm on `wasm32-unknown-unknown`. These are different implementations with different last-ULP rounding. They will not agree bit-for-bit.
- `math/probit.rs:41,68` and `heckman.rs:51,60` call `statrs` `Normal::cdf` (erf-based) — same class of cross-platform divergence anywhere a p-value/CDF reaches serialized output.
- The RIF quantile path (In-Scope 12, the founder's headline upgrade) runs *through* `calculate_rif` → `exp`+`powf`, so the quantile `decompose` JSON is the *most* exposed surface.

Consequence: during `/build`, AC-6 fails not because of RNG or threads but because `exp`/`erf` differ native↔wasm. Note the internal inconsistency: statistical-trust-layer's `parity_test.rs:24` already uses `TOLERANCE = 1e-6` (not byte-equality) for R/Stata parity — the correct posture — while deterministic-rng demands sha256 equality for native↔wasm.

Important: this does **not** weaken the actual threading-safety guarantee. Intra-platform, same thread-count-invariance (native-1t==native-2t==native-4t; wasm-seq==wasm-1t==wasm-2t==wasm-4t) *is* achievable and *is* the property that makes threading safe — the RNG+ordering fix delivers exactly that. Only the native↔wasm equality is the overreach.

**Recommendation (gate, no re-research):** split INV-02/AC-6 into (a) byte-identity *within* a platform across thread counts — the load-bearing threading criterion, keep sha256; (b) native↔wasm parity — reframe to tolerance-based (adopt statistical-trust-layer's `1e-6`/`1e-9`), not sha256. This aligns the two specs and makes the master gate satisfiable.

### MAJOR-2 — Reproducibility tension: AC-12/AC-16 require *cross-machine* build-std byte-equality that Open Item 5 says is unproven; this is the real cost of Strategy B over A

toolchain-build **AC-12/AC-16** require CI (`ubuntu-latest`) to recompute the raw-wasm sha256 and match `engine/pay_equity_engine.wasm.sha256`, which is built and committed on David's dev box (CachyOS). That is a **cross-machine** equality assertion. Yet the same spec's **Open Item 5** states cross-machine/two-host equality is *not* proven here and is "owned by the verification-benchmark suite." These are in direct tension: AC-12 makes CI red on every run if dev-host and CI-host build-std outputs diverge.

Strategy B is where this bites. B makes **std part of the hashed unit** (`-Zbuild-std` recompiles std from source), and *retires the existing, already-passing stable `--target bundler` baseline*. build-std cross-machine byte-reproducibility (codegen-unit ordering, embedded std source paths, toolchain-component identity across hosts) is a famously fragile property — materially harder than the pre-threading stable build ever had to satisfy. D7's claim that B "halves the reproducible surface" counts *artifacts*, not *difficulty*: one artifact that now hashes recompiled std is not obviously easier to hold reproducible than two artifacts that don't.

Directly answering the lens question **"is B genuinely lower-risk than A for reproducibility?"**: for operational simplicity, yes (one baseline, one pkg, one CI block, one loader branch). For **reproducibility robustness, not clearly** — B introduces the cross-machine build-std determinism risk that A structurally avoids by keeping an untouched, proven stable baseline as the defensibility artifact. For a client-facing pay-equity tool where the baseline *is* a defensibility artifact, that is a real argument for A that D7 undersells.

Sub-point reinforcing the fragility: the committed pin `nightly-2024-08-02` (native pin is `rust-toolchain.toml:5` = `1.90.0`, ~13 months *newer*). The Aug-2024 nightly must compile polars 0.44 + clarabel 0.11.1 + nalgebra 0.32 under build-std; several of those likely have an MSRV above that nightly's stable-equivalent (~1.80). E1's forward-bump will almost certainly move the pin, so the committed baseline hash is provisional until E1 lands — the reproducibility model's concrete anchor is unresolved at spec time (correctly deferred, but worth naming as risk, not a settled fact).

**Recommendation (gate + Phase-0 preflight):** before ratifying B, (1) generate the committed baseline **inside the CI environment / a pinned container** rather than on the dev box, OR (2) run a two-host build-std double-build preflight and only commit to B if it holds; (3) explicitly weigh A's untouched-stable-baseline as a defensibility asset, not just "two builds = more cost." Do not treat AC-12 cross-machine equality as free.

### MINOR-1 — thread-cap 2-term vs 3-term formula still inconsistent post-fix (RESOLUTION overstates "internally consistent")

memory-budget **AC-M8**: worker computes `N = min(N_max_const, hardwareConcurrency, 8)` and this domain writes the **unclamped** `N_max_const` into `thread-cap.js`. meridian **D2 worked code** (line 105): `cap = Math.min(navigator.hardwareConcurrency || 1, MEMORY_THREAD_CAP)` — 2-term, silently dropping the `8` ceiling; meridian's own "Not owned here" prose (line 40) says the formula is 3-term. The audit flagged this as MINOR in §5; the RESOLUTION fixed only MINOR-1 (signature line) and MINOR-2 (intercept line), and claims the set is "internally consistent" (line 180) — but this seam was not fixed. Harmless while `N_max_const ≤ 8` (given `N_target` band 4-8), but it is a latent contract mismatch that becomes live if any profile/N_target yields `N_max_const > 8`. **Fix:** either bake `min(_,8)` into `N_max_const` at emission and drop the `,8` from prose, or add `, 8` to the worker `Math.min`. State which, once.

### MINOR-2 — memory is non-binding at 50k; the "thread cap from memory budget" resolves to the hardcoded `8`

The formulas are rigorous but the arithmetic makes the constraint slack. Dataset ≈ 4 MB (memory-budget line 24). Post-refactor `Sc ≈ ~1F + regression matrices` ≈ ~12 MB; 8 threads ≈ `H_res + 8·(Sc+St)` ≈ ~100 MB, well inside the 256–512 MiB envelope. `N_max = floor((M_max−H_res−Marg)/(Sc+St))` with `M_max` sized from `N_target` recovers `≈ N_target`, so `N = min(N_max_const, hwConcurrency, 8)` binds on `hwConcurrency`/`8`, not on memory. INV-05 ("capped by the memory budget, not just hardwareConcurrency") is *formally* satisfied by the formula but *practically* the memory term never binds at 50k. Implication for founder decision (b) H2 margins: it is a low-stakes ratification at this scale — over-investing headroom analysis for a non-binding constraint. The profile is still worth building (extrapolation past 50k / ASM-04 invalidation), but the gate should be calibrated that memory is not actually the limiting factor at the stated ceiling.

### MINOR-3 — `seed_from_entropy()` on wasm is under-specified

deterministic-rng D1 (line 51) claims "no manifest change needed for entropy" and calls `getrandom::getrandom(&mut buf)` from a new `oaxaca_blinder/src/rng.rs`. But `oaxaca_blinder/Cargo.toml` has **no** `getrandom` dependency; only `engine/Cargo.toml:18,46` declares getrandom 0.3 with `getrandom/wasm_js`, and `rand 0.8` pulls getrandom **0.2**. Calling `getrandom::getrandom` from `oaxaca_blinder` requires a direct dep with the `js`/`wasm_js` feature for the relevant version. Off the default reproducible path (default = fixed `DEFAULT_SEED`, needs no entropy), so severity is low, but **AC-5** (entropy round-trip) may fail to link on wasm without the feature. Name the getrandom dep + feature or scope `seed_from_entropy()` to native.

### OBSERVATION — founder decision (c) MM→RIF aggregate switch orphans the MM-seeding work; deterministic-rng doesn't reconcile

Verified `engine/src/analysis.rs:~168` currently builds `QuantileDecompositionBuilder` (MM simulation) and returns `Vec::new()` detail (`:201-204`). The RESOLUTION rewires In-Scope 12 to `OaxacaBuilder::decompose_quantile()` (`builder.rs:720`, RIF-OLS → `run()` → the *mean* bootstrap path at `builder.rs:825`). If the founder picks the MM→RIF **aggregate** switch, the WASM quantile branch no longer executes `quantile_decomposition.rs`'s MM τ-grid/resample loop — yet deterministic-rng D4 + build-steps 5-7 spend real effort seeding exactly those sites (`:215,:244,:337-354`). That work becomes load-bearing only for native/MCP/CLI callers, and the WASM quantile bit-identity leg of AC-6 flows through `builder.rs:825`, not `quantile_decomposition.rs`. Not wrong (MM stays reachable), but the two specs don't state the post-switch division. The gate should note this so decision (c) doesn't leave deterministic-rng's MM section mis-scoped relative to the WASM tool.

## Verdict

**SHIP_WITH_FIXES.** The threading/memory/RNG engineering is fundamentally sound: schedule-independent seeding, index-vector resampling, sequential summation, COOP/COEP isolation, single-owner thread-cap, and the on-host double-build are all correct and verified against source. Two MAJORs must be resolved at the buildability gate *before* `/build` dispatch — but both are acceptance-criterion reframes + one preflight, **not** re-research: (1) relax the native↔wasm leg of INV-02/AC-6 from sha256 byte-identity to tolerance parity (transcendental libm divergence makes byte-identity unachievable cross-platform), keeping intra-platform byte-identity as the true threading gate; (2) resolve the AC-12 cross-machine build-std reproducibility tension (build the baseline in a pinned CI/container env or preflight two-host equality) and re-weigh Strategy A's untouched-stable baseline for its defensibility value rather than accepting B's "lower-risk" framing at face value. The three MINORs and the decision-(c) observation are gate-notes, not blockers.
